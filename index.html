The Thesis

State should be computed, not reconstructed.

A language model asked to play a character re-reads a transcript each turn and infers who it is supposed to be. That inference is expensive, inconsistent, and quietly lossy — a summary regenerated a few times will smooth a betrayal into "the contempt is gone."

Samskriti takes the opposite approach. Experiences transform state through a deterministic transition function; the state decides how the next experience lands; the model, when there is one, only speaks from what it is handed. The character persists because the state persists.

The state model comes from classical Indian accounts of how inner life changes — rasa, guna, samskara, vasana. That grounding isn't decorative. Those traditions describe states arising, colouring one another, fading at different rates, and hardening through repetition into disposition. That is a dynamical system, described long before there was notation for one.

Research

Character Without Retraining.

Emergent psychology from continuous emotional state dynamics in a non-LLM multi-agent simulation.

Three findings, none of them specified in advance — all emergent from the dynamics:

Bond strengths spanning orders of magnitude. Relationships differentiate on their own; nothing assigns them tiers.

A trimodal distribution of inherited traits. Descendants cluster into three temperamental groups without any rule producing three of anything.

Population-level shifts after loss. Grief propagates through the bond graph and moves the constitution of agents who were never directly involved.

Currently measuring where the claim holds. In one run, a rolling LLM summary — the standard way systems carry relationship context — erased a rupture entirely after three regenerations. Computed state retained it. Understanding that gap, including the parts that don't favour my own hypothesis, is the work.

The reference implementation for the paper. A 23-dimensional emotional state with triguna sensitivity, inter-rasa coupling, compound emergence, and habit formation from accumulated impressions.
