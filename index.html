Character is computed,
not configured.

Most artificial characters are written down — you author a personality, and it stays that description until you rewrite it. I build systems where character is the residue of what an agent has actually lived through.

Samskriti 23-dimensional state
188 bytes. No model. No network.

A model asked to play a character re‑reads a transcript and guesses who it was. State should be computed, not reconstructed.

The problem

Reconstruction is lossy in a specific, measurable way. A rolling summary regenerated a few times will smooth a betrayal into "the contempt is gone" — the words an actual summariser produced in one of my runs. The relationship survives in the transcript and disappears from the character.

How it works

Experience transforms state.
State decides what happens next.

01

Deterministic transition

An event's magnitude is derived from the state receiving it, never looked up in a table. An agent already carrying fear receives the next threat harder than a calm one does. Same input, different outcome, decided entirely by history.

02

Feelings interact

Wrath suppresses peace. Courage counters fear. Envy corrodes love. Eighteen coupling relationships run every tick, so moods compose instead of sitting in separate buckets waiting to be read.

03

Some states can only be grown

Despair, endurance and detachment have no event that produces them. They form when other states stay elevated together, long enough. You cannot hand a character despair — only a life that produces it.

04

Temperament itself drifts

The constitution deciding how hard everything lands is reshaped by what has already landed. An agent's sensitivity at hour fifty is a product of its first forty‑nine. That loop is why character compounds instead of merely accumulating.

The state model is drawn from classical Indian accounts of how inner life changes — rasa, guna, samskara, vasana. Not decoration: those traditions describe states arising, colouring one another, fading at different rates and hardening through repetition into disposition. That is a dynamical system, described a long time before there was notation for one.

Published research

Character Without Retraining.

Emergent psychology from continuous emotional state dynamics in a non‑LLM multi‑agent simulation. Three findings, none specified in advance.

  1. Bond strengths spanning orders of magnitude

    Relationships differentiate on their own. Nothing assigns them tiers.

  2. A trimodal distribution of inherited traits

    Descendants cluster into three temperamental groups, though no rule produces three of anything.

  3. Population-level shifts after loss

    Grief propagates through the bond graph and moves agents who were never directly involved.
